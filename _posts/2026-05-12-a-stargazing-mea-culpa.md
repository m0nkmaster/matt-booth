This is for anyone to read, but the timing is directed at my NZ chums.

“Regret the things you haven’t done rather than the things you have.” Good advice which I’ve tried to heed.

For just over a year, one regret has nagged at me. Borne out of lack of curiosity on my part well over a decade ago, it could have provided me with a regular gamut of joy. Not to mention something to do in the winter months.

I didn't try harder at looking up.

Why the dullest mea culpa outside a trainspotters club?

Because somewhere there is someone who is in the same position I was in: someone who wants to take an interest in all that is up there, lives in one of the *best* places in the world for it, but doesn’t realise just how accessible (cheap) it is.

It is nothing short of incredible the kind of photos we can take with our phones. Often with bits of kit that have been sat in a drawer for years. They needn’t be new, expensive, or even have an astrophotography mode. They just need to keep still.

If your phone can take a 15 or 20 second exposure and go to 800 or 1000 ISO, there’s no reason you can’t come up with pictures like I’ve managed in the last few weeks.

And now is a fantastic time to try:

Tonight and tomorrow there’s going to be some truly clear skies in Otago, and at the moment the Milky Way stretches in a big arc from west to east, with the core and the richest colours sitting in the eastern sky. It’s nothing new - it’s been doing this for billions of years after all - but it might be the first time you’ve thought to take a photo of it. And since you live right by a region considered to have zero light pollution (which, in the developed world, is pretty bloody rare), why not?

So do me a favour: go and find a quiet spot (without a mountain totally in the way), prop your phone up looking east/southeast, and take ten photos without moving it.

If you have a mobile tripod, fantastic. If you don't, you can keep your phone still with a mug and three bamboo skewers. Or a hairbrush. Or a fence. Or a shoe (take the foot out first).

If you have a Bluetooth clicker, that will make things easier. Some phones will even take a photo if you say cheese. What fun.

Then afterwards, a simple program can stack the images for you.

Your kids might end up with a new interest, you might have a mid-life-crisis hobby, who knows?!

Let me know how you get on, and thanks for coming to my Ted Talk.
